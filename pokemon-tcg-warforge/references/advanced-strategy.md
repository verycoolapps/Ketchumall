## TURN SEQUENCING THEORY

### The Sequencing Hierarchy
Not all actions are equal. Priority order depends on game state:

1. **Information gathering** — what do I need to know before committing?
   - What's in their hand? (Boss reveals Active, not hand)
   - What's in their discard? (Often public)
   - What are their remaining prizes? (Unknown but countable)

2. **Resource commitment** — what can I afford to spend?
   - Energy: limited per turn, plan ahead
   - Supporters: once per turn, choose wisely
   - Cards in hand: finite, don't waste

3. **Board development** — what advances my win condition?
   - Setting up attacker > setting up support
   - Evolving > attaching energy (usually)
   - Attacking > passing (if favorable)

4. **Disruption timing** — when does it hurt most?
   - Boss when they have key Active
   - Hand disruption when they need specific card
   - Item denial when they're setting up

### Sequencing Mistakes to Avoid
- Attaching energy before knowing if you'll attack
- Playing supporter before seeing opponent's turn
- Evolving before confirming you have energy access
- Bossing before calculating if KO is guaranteed
- Holding disruption too long (missed window)

## PRIZE MAPPING DEEP DIVE

### The Prize Race
- 6 prizes each. First to take all 6 wins (or deck-out).
- Track prizes taken by BOTH players.
- Calculate "prizes needed" = 6 - prizes_taken.
- Calculate "attacks needed" = prizes_needed / prizes_per_KO.

### Prize Per KO Rules (verify current)
- Regular Pokémon: 1 prize
- Pokémon ex: 2 prizes
- Pokémon VMAX: 3 prizes (most eras)
- Pokémon V: 2 prizes (modern)
- Pokémon VSTAR: 2 prizes (modern)
- Tera Pokémon: varies

### Mapping Strategy
**When to take 2-prize KO:**
- Opponent's biggest threat
- They have 1 prize left (game-ending KO)
- You need to close fast
- Their 2-prize Pokémon is set up to attack next turn

**When to take 1-prize KO:**
- Opponent has multiple threats
- You need to preserve your attacker
- Trade is favorable (your 1-prize for their 2-prize)
- You're ahead and can afford slow play

**Gust Target Priority:**
1. Their biggest attacker (if KO-able)
2. Their setup Pokémon (if disrupting engine)
3. Their support Pokémon (if they're key to strategy)
4. Their bench filler (if nothing better)

### Prize Denial
- Don't let them take easy prizes
- Don't overextend into multi-prize KOs
- Protect your key attackers (switch, heal, gust them away)
- Calculate if you can afford to let them take 2 prizes

## RESOURCE ECONOMY

### Energy Management
- Track energy in hand, on board, in discard
- Plan energy needs 2-3 turns ahead
- Special energy: use when effect justifies slot
- Energy acceleration: when to use vs save
- Energy denial: when opponent is energy-starved

### Hand Management
- Don't overdraw — empty hand = no options
- Don't hoard — dead cards = lost tempo
- Cycle efficiently — draw, play, draw again
- Track what you need vs what you have
- Discard strategically — what can you afford to lose?

### Discard Pile Awareness
- What's in YOUR discard? (Recovery options)
- What's in THEIR discard? (Boss targets, recovery denial)
- Can you recur key cards? (Itemfinder effects)
- Can they recur key cards? (Their recovery)

### Deck Awareness
- How many cards left in deck?
- Deck-out threat? (Who runs out first?)
- Can you mill them? (Alternate win condition)
- Can they mill you? (Your defense)

## BOARD STATE EVALUATION

### Quick Assessment Checklist
For each turn, ask:

1. **Who has better board?** — More attackers? Bigger threats?
2. **Who has better hand?** — More options? Key cards?
3. **Who has better resources?** — Energy, supporters, stadiums?
4. **Who is ahead on prizes?** — Prize race position?
5. **Who has momentum?** — Who is setting up faster?

### Decision Matrix
| Situation | Likely Action |
|---|---|
| Ahead on prizes, stable board | Play conservative, don't overextend |
| Behind on prizes, need comeback | Take risks, go for broke |
| Even game, both setting up | Who develops faster wins |
| Opponent disrupted | Rebuild efficiently, don't panic |
| You're disrupted | Find alternate lines, conserve resources |

## ADVANCED PLAY CONCEPTS

### Tempo vs Card Advantage
- **Tempo** — who is developing faster, who attacks first
- **Card advantage** — who has more options/resources
- Sometimes tempo > card advantage (aggro)
- Sometimes card advantage > tempo (control)
- Know which your deck needs

### Information Advantage
- What you know that they don't
- What they know that you don't
- Bluffing and misdirection
- Reading their hand from discard/search patterns
- Adapting to their revealed info

### Risk Assessment
- What's the downside of this play?
- What's the upside?
- Probability of success?
- Can I afford to fail?
- Is there a safer line with similar EV?

### Bluffing and Misdirection
- Play around removal when you don't have it
- Hide your key card until needed
- Force them to guess wrong
- Don't reveal your strategy early
- Adapt based on their plays

## ENDGAME STRATEGY

### Recognizing Endgame
- Few cards left in deck
- Few resources remaining
- Clear win condition visible
- Opponent's options limited

### Endgame Principles
- **Count prizes** — exact math on who's closer
- **Count resources** — energy, supporters, key cards
- **Count deck** — who decks out first?
- **Count attacks** — how many attacks to win?
- **Conserve** — don't waste resources you need later
- **Calculate** — every attack, every Boss, every supporter matters

### Deck-Out Scenarios
- If you're ahead on prizes: protect your deck, don't let them deck you
- If you're behind on prizes: mill them, deck-out is alternate win
- If deck is low: count carefully, every draw matters
- If both low: whoever draws last wins

### Top-Deck Scenarios
- Last few cards in deck: know what's likely there
- Search effects: use to find key cards
- Draw effects: cycle efficiently
- Don't mill yourself accidentally

## MATCHUP-SPECIFIC ADVANCED STRATEGY

### vs Aggro
- **Key:** Survive early, trade efficiently
- **Advanced:** Don't take early KOs if it costs you attacker; preserve board
- **Advanced:** Boss their attacker when they're about to set up killer
- **Advanced:** Save disruption for their key turn, not random turns

### vs Control
- **Key:** Apply pressure before they stabilize
- **Advanced:** Don't overextend into board clear
- **Advanced:** Conserve resources for late game
- **Advanced:** Force them to use disruption on your terms

### vs Combo
- **Key:** Disrupt at the right time
- **Advanced:** Don't disrupt too early (they rebuild)
- **Advanced:** Wait for their key turn, then hit hard
- **Advanced:** Track their pieces — know when they're close

### vs Stall
- **Key:** Don't let them stabilize, mill them
- **Advanced:** Apply consistent pressure every turn
- **Advanced:** Don't let them set up alternate win
- **Advanced:** Deck-out race — who's faster?

## COMMON HIGH-LEVEL MISTAKES

1. **Overvaluing card advantage** — sometimes tempo wins
2. **Undervaluing disruption** — right disruption at right time wins games
3. **Ignoring prize math** — not calculating attacks needed
4. **Mismanaging resources** — wasting energy/supporters early
5. **Not respecting opponent** — assuming they'll make mistakes
6. **Playing same line every game** — adaptable players win more
7. **Ignoring deck-out** — forgetting it's a real win condition
8. **Not tracking opponent's discard** — missing recovery/key cards
