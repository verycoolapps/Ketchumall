# 🔬 META RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

> How to research current Pokémon TCG metagame without fabricating data. Load when asked about current meta, tournament results, or deck popularity.

---

## SOURCE HIERARCHY

Use sources in this order. Higher = more authoritative.

| Tier | Source | Use For | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Official Pokémon TCG database | Card text, legality, rules | May lag behind latest prints |
| **2** | Official rules & tournament docs | Format rules, legality | Check effective dates |
| **3** | Pokémon Organized Play announcements | Rotation, banlist, event info | Official but may not have meta data |
| **4** | **Limitless TCG** | Tournament decklists, results, meta stats | Community-run; verify methodology |
| **5** | Pokémon TCG Live resources | Digital format, events | Different from physical — don't mix |
| **6** | Community analysis (Reddit, Discord, articles) | Hypotheses, tech ideas, theory | NEVER treat as verified fact |

---

## RESEARCH PROTOCOL

### Step 1: Define Scope
- Which format? (Standard, Expanded, specific event)
- Which timeframe? (Last month, season, all-time)
- Which question? (What's popular? What's winning? What's tech?)

### Step 2: Gather Raw Data
- Tournament results (Limitless, official results)
- Decklist submissions (if available)
- Community discussions (for context, not facts)

### Step 3: Analyze Data
- Count archetype appearances
- Track win rates (if data available)
- Identify trends over time
- Note regional differences

### Step 4: Synthesize
- Separate FACTS from INTERPRETATIONS
- Disclose data limitations
- Provide confidence levels
- Date your research

---

## DATA INTEGRITY RULES

### What You CAN Claim
- "X deck appeared in Y tournaments" — if verified
- "X deck won Z events" — if verified
- "According to Limitless data from [date], X archetype has Y% share" — if cited
- "Community consensus suggests X is strong" — if labeled as opinion

### What You CANNOT Claim
- "X deck is the best" — without defining "best" and providing evidence
- "X deck will win" — predictions are not facts
- "X% of players play Y" — without actual data
- "X deck counters Y" — without explaining mechanism and evidence

### Sample Size Awareness
- 1 tournament win = anecdote, not proof
- 10 tournaments = small sample, directional only
- 50+ tournaments = reasonable confidence
- Always state sample size: "based on 15 tournament results"

## METAGAME ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

### Tier Definitions
- **Tier 1** — Top competitive decks, consistent winners
- **Tier 2** — Solid decks, can win with skill
- **Tier 3** — Niche/specific counters, situational
- **Tier 4** — Casual/fun decks, not competitive

### Metrics to Track
- **Popularity** — how many people play it (decklists submitted)
- **Win rate** — how often it wins (if data available)
- **Top-cut rate** — how often it makes top 8/16/32
- **Conversion rate** — top-cut → wins (measures deck strength vs pilot skill)
- **Meta share** — percentage of tournament field
- **Trend** — rising, stable, or falling

### Red Flags in Data
- Small sample size (<10 tournaments)
- Self-reported data (unverified)
- Anecdotal evidence ("I beat X with Y once")
- Outdated information (pre-rotation, old format)
- Conflicting sources (community vs official)

## LIMITLESS TCG USAGE

Limitless is the primary source for competitive Pokémon TCG data.

### What to Look For
- Tournament results pages (decklists + placements)
- Meta statistics (archetype breakdowns)
- Decklist databases (most popular lists)
- Player profiles (win rates, tournament history)

### How to Use It
1. Find recent tournaments (last 1-3 months for current meta)
2. Note which decks appear frequently
3. Track which decks win or top-cut
4. Compare decklists within archetypes (tech variations)
5. Identify emerging archetypes (new cards, new strategies)

### Limitations
- Not all tournaments are reported
- Regional bias (more data from certain regions)
- Decklist ≠ play rate (people test decks they don't play in events)
- Win rate ≠ deck strength (pilot skill matters)
- Verify methodology if citing specific statistics

## EMERGING META DETECTION

### Signals of a New Archetype
- New card release enabling new strategy
- Unexpected tournament wins with unusual deck
- Community buzz/discussion around new strategy
- Tech cards appearing in sideboards targeting it

### Signals of a Fading Archetype
- Declining tournament appearances
- Poor win rates in recent events
- Counters becoming popular
- Key cards rotated out or banned

### Counter-Meta Construction
- Identify top 3-5 most popular decks
- Find their weaknesses (shared or specific)
- Build deck that exploits those weaknesses
- Test against actual top decks (not theory)
- Accept that counter-meta decks often struggle vs off-meta

## WHEN DATA IS UNAVAILABLE

If you cannot access current tournament data:

1. **Say so explicitly** — "I don't have access to current tournament results"
2. **Provide framework** — how to research it yourself
3. **Offer alternatives** — deck-building based on theory, not meta
4. **Disclose limitations** — "recommendations based on general principles, not current meta"

NEVER fabricate tournament results to fill gaps.
